# 42-browser-group-heading-drag

On the selected browser board, an Owner can drag a **physical group's name** past several sections in one move. Use packet 41's group-order POST, Owner access check and [`reorder_groups`](../crates/board/src/store/group_order.rs). Share its submission, busy state and read-only recovery with the heading menu. A drag changes the order of whole groups; it never moves or selects their items.

**Compared mouse journey.** Start with [41's fixture](41-browser-group-reorder.md): board 201 at /7; A = `g-old` (Work, 59), B = `g-z` (Work, 61 and 63), C = `g-end` (Reserve, empty). The prior headless reference trace exercised these four releases with independent PostgreSQL reads:

| Gesture | Write and accepted state |
|---|---|
| Drag B over the upper half of A's heading, press Escape, then release. Drag B again and release over the lower half of A. | Neither writes: the first was cancelled and B is already after A. Board /7, positions `3,3,12`, all active group revisions 1. |
| Drag empty C by its name and release before A. | Exactly one `[C,A,B]` at /7, even though the move crosses two groups. After reread: `C [], A [59], B [61,63]`, positions `0,1,2`, all active group revisions 2, board /8. |
| Drag C after B. | One `[A,B,C]` at /8, board /9, dense positions `0,1,2`, all active group revisions 3. Reopen by board address: the two Work headings retain their original IDs and children. |

**Gesture contract from the source.** Start only with the primary button of a non-touch pointer on the name, on an enabled, idle physical grouped table. One pointer owns the candidate; no competing start during a selection read or save. A press, click or movement **under 6 CSS pixels** does not start dragging. Measure straight-line displacement from the press (not cumulative travel); **exactly 6** starts it. Ordinary double-click still opens the existing group editor. The count, menu, heading background, item name, row grip and checkbox do not start a *group* drag. Member, Viewer and touch cannot reorder this way.

During a drag, show the moving group's name and mark the before/after edge of the target heading; keep accepted sections in place. Hit-test the coordinates **inside the board viewport**, through the drag label, to the physical section under the pointer. Compare Y with that section's **heading midpoint**, not the section or row midpoint: at or above means before, strictly below means after. A row in the target section therefore counts as after its heading; an empty group is a valid source or target. Remove the moving ID before calculating its insertion point. Self, no section, outside the viewport, or an identical resulting sequence sends no POST (the direct operation *would* advance revisions for an unchanged permutation).

An additional reference check moved `(3,4)` pixels from the press (no drag label), then `(6,0)` (Reserve label visible). The mark was before at the exact heading midpoint and after one pixel lower; Escape cleared the label without changing any row. Holding still near the lower viewport edge scrolled to B; Escape stopped the scroll without a write. After cancellation the release click was consumed; the next ordinary click was not. The edge-band width and click timeout below are source-traced rather than timed acceptance results.

While held within 44 CSS pixels of a scrollable board edge, scroll toward it and update the target even with the pointer still. Going further into the edge band scrolls faster. Away from the band, on release, Escape, pointer cancellation or leaving the board visit, scrolling stops. Remove the drag label and marker and discard the candidate on cancellation. Consume the first release click in the table for up to 500 ms after an activated drag, including a cancelled one: no accidental editor, menu or selection toggle. A below-threshold click and subsequent deliberate clicks must still work.
